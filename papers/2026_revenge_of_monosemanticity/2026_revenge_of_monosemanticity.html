Abstract 要旨

Understanding how neural networks learn and organize features is central to understanding their behavior. Much existing theory of feature learning has focused on the emergence of a global low-dimensional predictive geometry. We show that this picture is incomplete. In regression problems with clustered data, we demonstrate that multilayer perceptrons (MLPs) naturally develop monosemantic specialized neurons: individual neurons become strongly aligned with a specific predictive feature relevant to a particular region of the input space. Rather than learning a single global low-dimensional representation, MLPs learn a collection of local low-dimensional representations that can collectively span a high-dimensional space. This specialization provably gives MLPs a dataefficiency advantage over feature-learning methods based on a global lowdimensional representation.
ニューラルネットワークがどのように学習し、特徴を構成するかを理解することは、その挙動を把握する上で極めて重要です。特徴学習に関する既存の理論の多くは、大域的かつ低次元の予測幾何構造の創発に焦点を当ててきました。しかし、我々は、この見方が不完全であることを示します。クラスタ構造を持つデータを用いた回帰問題において、多層パーセプトロン（MLP）は、単一の意味（モノセマンティック）に対応する特化したニューロンを自然に形成することを示します。すなわち、個々のニューロンが、入力空間の特定の領域に関連する特定の予測特徴と強く整合するようになるのです。MLPは、単一の大域的な低次元表現を学習するのではなく、全体として高次元空間を張ることができるような、局所的な低次元表現の集合を学習します。このような特化により、MLPは大域的な低次元表現に基づく特徴学習手法と比較して、データ効率の面で有利であることが理論的に示されます。

1 Revisiting Feature Learning 特徴学習の再考

The empirical success of neural networks has motivated substantial work aimed at understanding what enables neural networks to outperform classical learning methods [Allen-Zhu and Li, 2019, Ghorbani et al., 2019, 2020a]. One prominent explanation is their ability to perform feature learning: rather than optimizing a predictor over a representation fixed before training, neural networks can use supervision to adapt their internal representations to the structure of the prediction task [Bengio et al., 2013].
ニューラルネットワークが経験的に成功を収めていることを背景に、なぜニューラルネットワークが従来の学習手法を凌駕できるのかを解明しようとする研究が活発に行われています [Allen-Zhu and Li, 2019, Ghorbani et al., 2019, 2020a]。その有力な説明の一つとして、特徴学習を行う能力が挙げられます。すなわち、ニューラルネットワークは、学習前に固定された表現に基づいて予測器を最適化するのではなく、教師信号を利用して、予測タスクの構造に合わせて内部表現を適応させることができるのです [Bengio et al., 2013]。

The distinction between fixed representations and feature learning can be formalized through the neural tangent kernel (NTK). Under a particular initialization scaling and in the infinite-width limit, neural-network training converges to kernel gradient descent with an NTK that remains fixed throughout training [Jacot et al., 2018, Lee et al., 2019]. In this regime, the neural network is effectively equivalent to a classical kernel method operating over a fixed representation. Away from this regime, however, the NTK can evolve substantially during training, and the resulting dynamics can no longer be described by a kernel fixed at initialization [Chizat et al., 2019, Woodworth et al., 2020, Geiger et al., 2020, Yang and Hu, 2021].
固定された表現と特徴学習との間の区別は、ニューラル・タンジェント・カーネル（NTK）を用いて定式化できます。特定の初期化スケーリングの下、かつネットワークの幅を無限大とする極限において、ニューラルネットワークの学習は、学習を通じて不変のNTKを用いたカーネル勾配降下法へと収束します [Jacot et al., 2018, Lee et al., 2019]。この領域（レジーム）では、ニューラルネットワークは実質的に、固定された表現に基づく従来のカーネル法と等価です。しかし、この領域から外れると、NTKは学習中に大きく変化し得るため、その結果生じるダイナミクスは、もは​​や初期化時に固定されたカーネルによっては記述できなくなります [Chizat et al., 2019, Woodworth et al., 2020, Geiger et al., 2020, Yang and Hu, 2021]。

This raises a fundamental question:
ここで、根本的な疑問が浮かび上がります。

When does such kernel evolution provide an advantage over methods based on fixed kernels?
そのようなカーネルの進化は、固定カーネルに基づく手法に対して、いつ利点をもたらすのでしょうか。

A major advance in understanding neural networks was the recognition that neural networks can exploit low-dimensional structure in the target, where prediction depends on only a small number of directions in the input space. In such settings, neural networks can adapt their representations to identify these predictive directions, whereas fixed-feature like kernel methods cannot [Bach, 2017, Soltanolkotabi, 2017, Yehudai and Shamir, 2019, Wei et al., 2019, Ghorbani et al., 2019, 2020b, Malach et al., 2021, Abbe et al., 2022, 2023, Ba et al., 2022, Damian et al., 2022, Dandi et al., 2024, Bruna and Hsu, 2025]. In this setting, the target takes the form
ニューラルネットワークの理解における大きな進展の一つは、 ニューラルネットワークが対象（ターゲット）の低次元構造、すなわち入力空間内の少数の方向のみに予測が依存するような構造を活かせるという認識でした。 そのような状況下では、ニューラルネットワークは表現を適応させて予測に寄与する方向を特定できますが、カーネル法のような固定特徴量を用いる手法ではそれができません [Bach, 2017, Soltanolkotabi, 2017, Yehudai and Shamir, 2019, Wei et al., 2019, Ghorbani et al., 2019, 2020b, Malach et al., 2021, Abbe et al., 2022, 2023, Ba et al., 2022, Damian et al., 2022, Dandi et al., 2024, Bruna and Hsu, 2025]。この設定において、対象は次のような形式をとります。 \[ f(x) = g\big(U^\top x\big),\quad U ∈ \mathbb{R}^{d×r},\quad r \ll d. \] Although \(x ∈ \mathbb{R}^d\), the response depends only on the \(r\)-dimensional predictive subspace spanned by the columns of \(U\).
\(x \in \mathbb{R}^d\) ではあるが、応答は \(U\) の列によって張られる \(r\) 次元の予測部分空間にのみ依存する。

A feature-learning method can use the responses of the model to learn the representations corresponding to the predictive subspace, effectively reducing the problem from the ambient dimension d to the intrinsic dimension r. By contrast, a representation fixed independently of the labels would still be dependent on \(d\) dimensions.
特徴学習手法では、モデルの応答を利用して予測部分空間に対応する表現を学習することで、問題を元の次元数 \(d\) から真の次元数 \(r\) へと効果的に縮約できます。対照的に、ラベルとは無関係に固定された表現では、依然として \(d\) 次元の影響を受けることになります。

Taken together, this line of work views feature learning primarily as a mechanism for discovering low-dimensional predictive structure: supervision reshapes the representation so that learning is governed by the intrinsic dimension of the task rather than the ambient dimension of the input.
これらを総合すると、一連の研究において、特徴学習は主として低次元の予測構造を発見するためのメカニズムであると捉えられています。すなわち、教師あり学習は、入力の本来の次元数（アンビエント次元）ではなく、タスクの本質的な次元数によって学習が支配されるように、表現を再構成するのです。

In our work, we identify a qualitatively different mechanism of feature learning in multilayer perceptrons (MLPs) that is not fundamentally tied to recovering a single global low-dimensional predictive subspace.
本研究において我々は、単一の広域的な低次元予測部分空間の復元とは根本的に結びついていない、多層パーセプトロン（MLP）における質的に異なる特徴学習メカニズムを特定する。

In this setting, the data are drawn from multiple clusters, and each cluster can have its own predictive directions and its own link function.
この設定では、データは複数のクラスタから抽出され、各クラスタは独自の予測方向とリンク関数を持ち得ます。

Thus, while the prediction problem could be low-dimensional locally within each cluster, there may be no lowdimensional structure globally.
したがって、各クラスタ内では局所的に低次元の予測問題となり得るものの、全体としては低次元の構造が存在しない可能性があります。

We show that MLPs can simultaneously discover the cluster structure and learn the predictive functions relevant to each cluster. This uncovers a new form of feature learning that goes beyond the standard paradigm of recovering a single global low-dimensional predictive subspace.
我々は、MLP（多層パーセプトロン）がクラスタ構造の発見と、各クラスタに関連する予測関数の学習を同時に行えることを示す。これは、単一の広域的な低次元予測部分空間を復元するという標準的なパラダイムを超えた、新たな形態の「特徴学習」を明らかにするものである。

Our main contributions are as follows.
我々の主な貢献は以下の通り

We show that in trained MLPs, a substantial fraction of individual neurons become monosemantic, specializing by aligning predominantly with a single cluster-specific predictive direction. This specialization allows the MLP to learn both the relevant local low-dimensional features and an implicit clustering that determines where each feature is useful. The resulting behavior resembles mixture-of-experts routing, but emerges within a standard MLP without an explicit routing module or expert decomposition.
学習済みMLPにおいて、個々のニューロンのかなりの割合が「単一の意味（モノセマンティック）」を持つようになり、特定のクラスタに対応する単一の予測方向と主に整合することで特化していくことを示します。この特化により、MLPは、関連する局所的な低次元特徴と、各特徴がどこで有用かを決定する暗黙的なクラスタリングの両方を学習することが可能になります。その結果生じる挙動は「Mixture-of-Experts（MoE）」におけるルーティングに似ていますが、明示的なルーティングモジュールやエキスパートへの分解を用いることなく、標準的なMLPの内部で自然に創発するものです。

While this specialization happens for standard ReLU and GeLU [Hendrycks and Gimpel, 2016], modern architectures with multiplicative gating, such as ReGLU and SwiGLU [Shazeer, 2020], can substantially improve sample efficiency in this setting.
標準的なReLUやGeLU [Hendrycks and Gimpel, 2016] においてもこうした特化は生じますが、ReGLUやSwiGLU [Shazeer, 2020] といった乗法的なゲーティング（multiplicative gating）を備えた現代的なアーキテクチャは、この設定においてサンプル効率を大幅に向上させることができます。

Furthermore, we show that this specialization leads to increasingly favorable sample-complexity scaling as the number of clusters grows, allowing MLPs to maintain strong performance even when the cluster-specific predictive directions collectively span the ambient space and no useful global low-dimensional structure exists.
さらに、クラスタ数が増加するにつれて、この特化がますます有利なサンプル複雑度のスケーリングをもたらすことを示します。これにより、クラスタ固有の予測方向が全体として埋め込み空間（アンビエント空間）を張り、かつ有用な大域的な低次元構造が存在しない場合であっても、MLPは高い性能を維持することが可能になります。

1.1 Discussion 考察

The form of feature learning identified in this work differs from the classical feature learning, where the main goal is to recover a single global lowdimensional predictive subspace. In such settings, methods designed for learning low-dimensional features can perform as well or better than MLPs. A prominent example is the Recursive Feature Machine (RFM), a supervised kernelbased method that uses input-response pairs to learn a global low-dimensional predictive subspace [Radhakrishnan et al., 2024, 2025]. RFM is therefore a particularly informative comparison: like an MLP, it learns features from the data rather than operating with a fixed representation.
本研究で特定された特徴学習の形態は、単一の広域的な低次元予測部分空間を復元することを主目的とする従来の特徴学習とは異なります。そのような設定においては、低次元特徴を学習するように設計された手法が、MLP（多層パーセプトロン）と同等、あるいはそれ以上の性能を発揮することがあります。その代表的な例がRecursive Feature Machine（RFM）です。これは、入力と応答のペアを用いて広域的な低次元予測部分空間を学習する、教師ありのカーネルベース手法です[Radhakrishnan et al., 2024, 2025]。したがって、RFMは特に有益な比較対象となります。なぜなら、RFMはMLPと同様に、固定された表現を用いるのではなく、データから特徴を学習するからです。

However, as the number of clusters increases, the span of cluster-specific predictive directions grows until it equals the ambient space, so they no longer lie in a single low-dimensional global subspace. This demonstrates a key advantage of MLPs – they can use neuron specialization to preserve the association between each predictive direction and the cluster in which it is relevant, allowing MLPs to remain effective as the number of clusters grows, while RFM feature learning ability diminishes with the number of clusters.
しかし、クラスター数が増加するにつれて、クラスター固有の予測方向が張る空間は拡大し、やがて埋め込み空間全体を覆うようになるため、それらはもはや単一の低次元な大域的部分空間には収まらなくなります。これはMLPの重要な利点を示しています。すなわち、MLPはニューロンの専門化を利用することで、各予測方向とそれに関連するクラスターとの対応関係を維持できるため、クラスター数が増加しても有効性を保てるのです。一方、RFMの素性学習能力は、クラスター数が増えるにつれて低下してしまいます。

To study this phenomenon systematically, we consider a mixture of single-index models. Suppose the data are drawn from K clusters. For an input \(x ∈ \mathbb{R}^d\) belonging to cluster \(c ∈ [K]\), the target is
この現象を体系的に研究するために、シングルインデックスモデルの混合モデルを考えます。データは \(K\) 個のクラスタから生成されると仮定します。クラスタ \(c ∈ [K]\) に属する入力 \(x ∈ \mathbb{R}^d\) に対して、ターゲットは \[ f(x) = g_c\big(v_c^\top x\big),\quad v_c ∈ \mathbb{R}^d \] Thus, prediction is one-dimensional within each cluster, but both the relevant direction \(v_c\) and the link function \(g_c\) may vary across clusters.
したがって、各クラスタ内での予測は一次元的であるが、関連する方向 \(v_c\) とリンク関数 \(g_c\) の双方は、クラスタ間で異なり得る。

The classical single-index model corresponds to \(K = 1\). When \(K \gt 1\), however, the predictive directions of different clusters may collectively span the entire ambient space:
古典的な単一指標モデルは \(K = 1\) に対応します。しかし、\(K > 1\) の場合、 異なるクラスタの予測方向が、全体として埋め込み空間（アンビエント空間）全体を 張る可能性があります。 \[ rank \big([v_1 ··· v_K]\big) = d. \] Hence, prediction can be low-dimensional within every cluster even though there is no useful global low-dimensional predictive subspace to recover.
したがって、抽出可能な有用な大域的低次元予測部分空間が存在しない場合であっても、各クラスタ内では予測を低次元で行うことが可能である。

1.2 Prior Work 先行研究

Feature learning of global low-dimensional structure. A large body of work studies feature learning for multi-index targets. These works have shown that neural-network models can adapt to an unknown low-dimensional subspace of the inputs, succeeding where fixed-kernel methods are sample-inefficient [Bach, 2017, Soltanolkotabi, 2017, Yehudai and Shamir, 2019, Ghorbani et al., 2020b]. Recently, the literature has characterized how gradient-based training succeeds in recovering this subspace for isotropic data [Abbe et al., 2022, 2023, Damian et al., 2022, Ba et al., 2022, Dandi et al., 2024, Damian et al., 2025, Bruna and Hsu, 2025], and in the related setting of classifying Gaussian-mixture data with a constant number of clusters [Refinetti et al., 2021, Ben Arous et al., 2024].
大域的な低次元構造の学習。 マルチインデックス・ターゲット（multi-index targets）に対する特徴学習については、数多くの研究が行われてきました。これらの研究により、ニューラルネットワーク・モデルは入力の未知の低次元部分空間に適応できることが示されています。これは、固定カーネル法ではサンプル効率が低い状況においても成功を収めるものです [Bach, 2017, Soltanolkotabi, 2017, Yehudai and Shamir, 2019, Ghorbani et al., 2020b]。近年では、等方性データに対して勾配法を用いた学習がいかにしてこの部分空間を復元できるかについて、理論的な解明が進んでいます [Abbe et al., 2022, 2023, Damian et al., 2022, Ba et al., 2022, Dandi et al., 2024, Damian et al., 2025, Bruna and Hsu, 2025]。また、固定数のクラスタを持つガウス混合データを分類するという関連設定においても、同様の解析が行われています [Refinetti et al., 2021, Ben Arous et al., 2024]。

However, in our work, we prove that neuron specialization is a different form of feature learning, allowing MLPs to efficiently tackle settings where a single global low-dimensional representation is insufficient.
しかし、我々の研究では、ニューロンの特化が一種の「特徴学習」であることを証明します。これにより、単一の低次元な大域的表現では不十分な状況においても、MLP（多層パーセプトロン）が効率的に対処できるようになります。

Neuron specialization. Emergence of specialized neurons has been observed in several settings. For instance, training neural networks to do modular arithmetic leads to specialized neurons each representing different Fourier components [Nanda et al., 2023, Gromov, 2023, Morwani et al., 2024, He et al., 2026]. When learning XOR-type targets, it has also been shown that neurons specialize to four clusters rather than being distributed evenly across a predictive low-dimensional subspace [Frei et al., 2023, Glasgow, 2024]. And in teacherstudent settings the student’s neurons have been shown to often align to the teacher’s neurons [Tian, 2020, Oostwal et al., 2021, Zhu et al., 2025], although this depends on initialization [Jarvis et al., 2025].
ニューロンの特化。 特化したニューロンの出現は、いくつかの状況で観察されています。例えば、モジュロ演算を行うようにニューラルネットワークを学習させると、それぞれが異なるフーリエ成分を表すような特化したニューロンが生じます [Nanda et al., 2023, Gromov, 2023, Morwani et al., 2024, He et al., 2026]。 XOR型のターゲットを学習する場合にも、ニューロンは予測に寄与する低次元部分空間全体に均一に分布するのではなく、4つのクラスタに特化することが示されています [Frei et al., 2023, Glasgow, 2024]。また、教師・生徒（teacher-student）設定においては、生徒側のニューロンが教師側のニューロンと整合するようになることがしばしば示されています [Tian, 2020, Oostwal et al., 2021, Zhu et al., 2025]（ただし、これは初期化に依存します [Jarvis et al., 2025]）。

These works mainly study specialization to globally relevant features or to cluster directions that themselves determine the target. In our work, neurons specialize to locally predictive features. Thus, an MLP must not only learn the predictive directions, but also preserve their association with the clusters in which they are relevant. Moreover, these works do not isolate a sample-complexity advantage of specialization over an adaptive feature-learning method based on a single global representation, such as the Recursive Feature Machine [Radhakrishnan et al., 2024]. We establish such an advantage: when cluster-specific predictive directions collectively span a high-dimensional space, neuron specialization allows an MLP to preserve these local feature-cluster associations and achieve better sample complexity.
これらの研究は主に、大域的に重要な特徴や、それ自体がターゲットを決定するようなクラスタの方向性への特化を扱っています。一方、本研究では、ニューロンが局所的に予測に寄与する特徴へと特化する点に着目します。そのため、MLP（多層パーセプトロン）は予測に役立つ方向性を学習するだけでなく、それらが関連するクラスタとの結びつきを維持する必要があります。さらに、既存研究では、単一の大域的表現に基づく適応的特徴学習手法（Recursive Feature Machine [Radhakrishnan et al., 2024] など）と比較した際の、特化による「サンプル複雑性（sample complexity）」上の利点は明確に示されていませんでした。本研究では、そのような利点を明らかにします。すなわち、クラスタ固有の予測方向性が全体として高次元空間を張る場合、ニューロンの特化によって、MLPは局所的な特徴とクラスタの関連性を維持し、より優れたサンプル複雑性を実現できるのです。

Monosemanticity. Interpretability works on language models have found that some individual neurons are monosemantic, responding primarily to a single interpretable concept or pattern, while others are polysemantic, responding to multiple distinct concepts or patterns [Bills et al., 2023, Elhage et al., 2022]. More recent work has argued that monosemantic features need not align with individual neurons, and has used sparse dictionary learning to recover more monosemantic feature directions from neural representations [Bricken et al., 2023, Templeton et al., 2024]. Our work shows that, at least in simple MLPs, monosemantic features can emerge directly at the level of individual neurons and that this neuron-level specialization can improve sample efficiency compared with methods that are limited to global feature discovery.
単義性（Monosemanticity）。 言語モデルの解釈可能性に関する研究により、個々のニューロンの中には、主に単一の解釈可能な概念やパターンに反応する「単一意味的（monosemantic）」なものがある一方で、複数の異なる概念やパターンに反応する「多義的（polysemantic）」なものも存在することが明らかになっています [Bills et al., 2023, Elhage et al., 2022]。より最近の研究では、単一意味的な特徴が必ずしも個々のニューロンと一致する必要はないと論じられており、スパース辞書学習を用いて、ニューラル表現からより単一意味的な特徴の方向を抽出する手法が用いられています [Bricken et al., 2023, Templeton et al., 2024]。本研究では、少なくとも単純なMLP（多層パーセプトロン）においては、単一意味的な特徴が個々のニューロンのレベルで直接的に創発し得ること、そしてこのニューロン・レベルでの特化が、全体的な特徴の発見にとどまる手法と比較して、サンプル効率を向上させ得ることを示します。

Mixture of Experts models. A complementary line of work studies specialization and latent-cluster recovery in mixture-of-experts (MoE) architectures. Chen et al. [2022] analyze a clustered classification problem and show that an MoE router can learn cluster-center features that partition the problem into simpler subproblems handled by different experts. Dikkala et al. [2023] likewise show, theoretically and empirically, that a learned router can route inputs according to latent clusters. Particularly close to our statistical setting, Kawata et al. [2025] study nonlinear regression with an underlying cluster structure of single-index models and show that an MoE trained by SGD can detect the latent organization and divide the task into cluster-specific subproblems; under their assumptions, a vanilla neural network does not detect this organization within their polynomial complexity regime.

4 Theory 理論

In this section, we prove two main results describing how MLPs learn data with cluster structure. In Theorem 1, we prove that neurons in the MLP specialize during training. In Theorem 2, we prove that, as the number of clusters tends to infinity, MLPs outperform standard kernel methods and Recursive Feature Machines (RFM) in terms of sample complexity. This comes as a consequence of the fact that the latter two methods cannot compute specialized features for each cluster.
本節では、クラスター構造を持つデータをMLP（多層パーセプトロン）がどのように学習するかを記述する2つの主要な結果を証明します。定理1では、学習の過程でMLP内のニューロンが特化していくことを証明します。定理2では、クラスター数が無限大に近づくにつれて、サンプル複雑性の観点からMLPが標準的なカーネル法やRecursive Feature Machines（RFM）を凌駕することを証明します。これは、後者の2つの手法が各クラスターに特化した特徴量を計算できないという事実に起因しています。

4.1 MLP Neurons Specialize When Learning on Gaussian Mixture Model Data
　ガウス混合モデルのデータで学習する際、MLPのニューロンは特化する

In this subsection, we show that, under small initialization, neurons trained on well-separated Gaussian mixture data specialize to cluster-specific predictive directions.
本小節では、初期値が小さい条件下において、十分に分離されたガウス混合データで学習されたニューロンが、各クラスタに特有の予測方向へと特化することを示します。

We consider \(K\)-cluster Gaussian mixture data in \(d = 2K\) dimensions. Let \(e_1, ..., e_K\) denote the standard basis of \(\mathbb{R}^K\). We use the first \(K\) coordinates to separate the cluster means and the last \(K\) coordinates for the cluster-specific predictive directions.1

1 Note that independent orthogonal changes of basis in the two blocks preserve the isotropic Gaussian noise and all conclusions below.

Data Setting 1 — Symmetric Gaussian mixture

Let \(R \gt 0\) be a cluster separation parameter and, for \(c ∈ [K]\), let

In the notation of Section 2, the data distribution is
第2節の表記法では、データ分布は \[ c \sim \text{Unif}([K]),\quad x|c \sim \mathcal{N}(µ_c, Σ_c),\quad y = g_c(\langle x, v_c\rangle ) = h_3(\langle x, v_c\rangle ). \]

Thus the routing coordinates encode cluster identity through the means \(Re_c\), where \(R\) should be thought of as a large cluster-separation parameter, so that the clusters are well separated, while within cluster \(c\) the response depends only on the cluster-specific predictive direction \(v_c\). The cubic Hermite target is chosen because there is an explicit expression for the expected product \(\mathbb{E}[y\phi(ω^\top x)]\) between the target response and the ReLU neuron’s activation, allowing us to characterize the directions to which neurons converge. Next, we consider training a neural network to learn this data distribution.
このように、ルーティング座標は \(Re_c\) という形式でクラスタの識別情報を符号化しています。ここで \(R\) は大きなクラスタ分離パラメータとみなされ、クラスタ同士は十分に離間しています。一方、クラスタ \(c\) の内部では、応答はクラスタ固有の予測方向 \(v_c\) にのみ依存します。三次エルミート関数をターゲットとして採用したのは、ターゲットとなる応答とReLUニューロンの活性化との積の期待値 \(\mathbb{E}[y\phi(ω^\top x)]\) について明示的な式が得られるためであり、これによりニューロンが収束する方向を特徴付けることが可能になります。次に、このデータ分布を学習するようにニューラルネットワークを訓練することを考えます。

Training Setup 1 — Two-layer ReLU population gradient flow

We train the two-layer ReLU network

Indeed, we prove that any positive mixed-cluster critical point has an unstable direction. Analytic-gradient-flow convergence and strict-saddle avoidance therefore imply that a random neuron specializes almost surely.

Permutation symmetry makes the selected cluster uniform on \([K]\), and independence of the initial hidden directions makes the selected labels independent. The coverage estimate is then the usual coupon-collector union bound. 4.2 A Sample-complexity Gap Between MLPs, Kernel Methods, and RFM
　MLP、カーネル法、およびRFMの間のサンプル複雑度のギャップ

In this subsection, we establish a sample-complexity separation between twolayer MLPs and both standard kernel methods and Recursive Feature Machines (RFM). We use a data distribution similar to that in the previous subsection, but with full separation between clusters for ease of analysis.
本小節では、2層MLPと、標準的なカーネル法およびRecursive Feature Machines（RFM）との間に、サンプル複雑度の観点での分離可能性（separation）があることを示します。ここでは、前小節と同様のデータ分布を用いますが、解析を容易にするために、各クラスタ間が完全に分離された設定とします。

Data Setting 2 — Gaussian mixture with noiseless routing

We set the means \(µ_c = [e_c; 0]\) and predictive directions \(v_c = [0; e_c]\) in \(d = 2K\) dimensions, but make the first \(K\) coordinates noiseless by setting

我々は一般的なクリップドランプリンク \(g_c = g\) を使用する。 \[ g(t) = t_+ − (t − 1)_+. \] Thus, in the notation of Section 2,

勾配に基づく更新によって学習されたネットワークを調べる代わりに、本研究では、重みに対するフロベニウスノルム制約の下で経験リスク最小化により適合させた2層MLPを検討する。

Training Setup 2 — Frobenius-constrained MLP ERM

We fit the two-layer ReLU network

本稿では、このMLP ERMを、標準的な回転不変カーネルリッジ回帰、および教師ありカーネル法であるRFM [Radhakrishnan et al., 2024, 2025] と比較します。RFMに対しては、真の母集団平均勾配外積（AGOP）を与えることで、計量推定誤差を排除し、単一のグローバルな特徴量計量を用いることの限界を切り分けて検証します。2

A Additional experimental details

A.1 Additional Details for MLPs Develop Specialized Firstlayer Neurons Experiments

Data generation. We use \(K = 10\) clusters in \(d = 40\) dimensions, with the cluster index \(c\) sampled uniformly from \(\{1, . . . , K\}\). We choose the cluster centers and predictive directions to lie along separate coordinate axes:
\[ [µ_c]_j = \begin{cases} 20, & j = c,\\ 0, & otherwise, \end{cases} \quad [v_c]_j = \begin{cases} 1, & j = K + c,\\ 0, & otherwise, \end{cases} \quad j ∈ \{1, . . . , d\}. \] For each cluster c, we sample
\[ x = µ_c + z,\quad z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_d/d). \] Thus, coordinates 1:10 encode cluster position, coordinates 11:20 contain the cluster-specific predictive directions \(\{v_c\}_{c=1}^K\), and coordinates 21:40 are nuisance coordinates.

We use the same normalized second-Hermite link in every cluster and add Gaussian observation noise:
\[ y =\frac{\text{He}_2\big(\sqrt{d}⟨v_c, x⟩\big)}{\sqrt2!}+\varepsilon,\quad \text{He}_2(t) = t^2 − 1,\quad \varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 0.005^2). \] Training, validation, and test sets are generated independently and contain 100,000, 4,096, and 2,048 examples, respectively.

Models and optimization. We train one-hidden-layer ReLU, GELU, ReGLU, and SwiGLU networks of width 2,048, with trainable hidden and output biases and balanced feature-learning initialization. For example, the hidden representation of a ReGLU network is
\[ \text{ReLU}(W_gx + b_g) \odot (W_vx + b_v). \] All models are trained with Adam under the corresponding width-aware parameterization, using a batch size of 2,048, cosine learning-rate decay, and zero weight decay.

For ReLU, we search initial learning rates
\[ \{0.004, 0.008, 0.012, 0.016, 0.024, 0.032\}, \] with each schedule decaying to one tenth of its initial value. Model selection uses validation MSE only. The selected ReLU schedule is 0.004 → 0.0004, with the validation-selected checkpoint at step 37,000. The schedules used for the remaining architectures are
\[ \begin{align} \text{GELU: } & 0.016 → 0.0016,\\ \text{ReGLU: } & 0.0016 → 0.00016,\\ \text{SwiGLU: } & 0.0256 → 0.00256. \end{align} \] GELU, ReGLU, and SwiGLU are trained for at most 20,000 steps, while the ReLU candidates are trained for at most 40,000 steps. For every model, we select the checkpoint with the smallest validation MSE before evaluating on the test set.

Active-neuron criterion. We restrict the specialization analysis to neurons that contribute materially to the network output. For an ordinary ReLU or GELU neuron, we define its importance as
\[ I_j = |a_j|||w_j||_2, \] where \(a_j\) is its output weight and \(w_j\) is its incoming weight. For a gated ReGLU or SwiGLU neuron, we use
\[ I_j = |a_j|||w_{g,j}||_2 ||w_{v,j}||_2, \] where \(w_{g,j}\) and \(w_{v,j}\) denote its gate and value weights.

We sort neurons by \(I_j\) and retain the smallest leading set accounting for at least 99.9% of the total importance. We refer to these retained neurons as active. For gated architectures, the same active-neuron set is used when analyzing the gate and value weights separately.

The 99.9% threshold is a descriptive sparsification rule rather than a statistical significance threshold. Its purpose is to prevent the specialization statistics from being dominated by neurons with negligible influence on the network output.

Specialization measures. For an incoming weight vector wj , we define its maximum absolute cosine similarity with the cluster-specific predictive directions as
\[ A_j = \max\limits_{1\leq c\leq K}\frac{|⟨w_j , v_c⟩|}{||w_j||_2} \tag{1} \] Because each vc is a unit vector, Aj is the largest absolute cosine similarity between wj and any cluster-specific predictive direction. A large value therefore indicates that a substantial fraction of the weight norm is concentrated along a single predictive direction.

Cosine alignment can be conservative when one predictive coordinate is much larger than every individual competing coordinate but the weight vector also contains many small coefficients. To capture this behavior, define
\[ \hat{c}_j ∈ \text{arg}\max\limits_{1\leq c\leq K}|⟨w_j , v_c⟩| \] and the predictive-coordinate dominance score
\[ D_j =\frac{|[w_j]_{K+\hat{c}_j}|}{\max\limits_{\substack{ 1\leq ℓ\leq d\\ℓ\neq K+\hat{c}_j}}|[w_j]_ℓ|}. \tag{2} \] Since the predictive direction vc is supported on coordinate \(K + c, D_j \gt 1\) means that the largest-magnitude coordinate of \(w_j\) is a predictive coordinate, while \(D_j \gt 2\) means that this predictive coordinate is more than twice as large as every other coordinate.

For ReGLU and SwiGLU, both \(A_j\) and \(D_j\) are computed separately for the gate weights \(w_{g,j}\) and value weights \(w_{v,j}\) . Absolute inner products are used throughout because alignment with \(v_c\) and \(−v_c\) represents the same predictive direction.

A.2 Additional Details for MLPs Jointly Learn Cluster Structure and Cluster-specific Predictive Functions

Cluster and predictive geometry. We choose d = 20 and divide the input into two ten-dimensional subspaces:
\[ \mathbb{R}^{20} = \mathcal{S}_{cluster} \oplus \mathcal{S}_{pred}. \]

Figure 5: Predictive-coordinate dominance complements cosine alignment. Coordinate-wise absolute weights of three active neurons. Each neuron has only moderate cosine alignment because its norm includes many smaller coefficients, but nevertheless exhibits a pronounced spike along one cluster-specific predictive direction \(v_c\). These examples illustrate why predictive-coordinate dominance captures specialization that can be missed by a strict cosine threshold.

The first contains information identifying the cluster, while the second contains the cluster-specific predictive signal. We consider \(K ∈ \{1, 2, 10, 50\}\) equally likely clusters.

For each \(K\), we construct well-separated cluster-center directions in \(\mathbb{R}^{10}\). We first draw
\[ L_K = \max\{20,000, 500K\} \] independent Gaussian vectors and normalize them to unit norm. We select the first direction uniformly from this collection and choose each subsequent direction to minimize its largest inner product with the directions already selected. This farthest-point procedure produces unit vectors
\[ s_1,...,s_K ∈ \mathbb{S}^9,\quad \mathbb{S}^9 = \{s ∈ \mathbb{R}^{10} : ||s||_2 = 1\}, \] with large pairwise separation. The candidate collection and selected directions are generated separately for each value of \(K\).

For \(K \gt 1\), define the largest pairwise similarity
\[ ρ_K = \max\limits_{c\neq c^\prime}s_c^\top s_{c^\prime} \] We set \(ρ_1 = 0\). The center of cluster \(c\) in the full ambient space is
\[ µ_c = \begin{bmatrix} s_c \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} ∈ \mathbb{R}^{20},\quad K \gt 1. \] For \(K = 1\), we set \(µ_1 = 0\), since no cluster identification is required.

The standard deviation of the noise in the cluster-identifying coordinates is
\[ σ_K =\frac{1 − ρ_K}{2γ},\quad γ = 4. \tag{3} \] Thus, the noise level is adjusted using the least-separated pair of cluster centers, maintaining clear cluster separation as \(K\) changes.

Each cluster also receives an independently and uniformly sampled unit direction in the predictive subspace,
\[ \tilde{v}_c \sim \text{Unif}(\mathbb{S}^9). \] We embed this direction into the full ambient space by zero-padding the clusteridentifying coordinates:
\[ v_c = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ \tilde{v}_c \end{bmatrix} ∈ \mathbb{R}^{20}. \] Thus, \(v_c\) lies entirely in \(\mathcal{S}_{pred}\), whereas \(µ_c\) lies entirely in \(\mathcal{S}_{cluster}\). Consequently, the coordinates identifying the cluster are disjoint from those determining its response.

Input distribution. The cluster identity C is sampled uniformly:
\[ C \sim \text{Unif}\{1,..., K\}. \] Conditional on \(C = c\), the input is
\[ x = µ_c + \begin{bmatrix} σ_Kz_{cluster}\\ z_{pred} \end{bmatrix},\quad z_{cluster}, z_{pred} \overset{iid}{\sim} \mathcal{N}(0, I_{10}). \tag{4} \] Equivalently, in the notation of Section 2,
\[ x|C = c \sim \mathcal{N}(µ_c, Σ_c),\quad Σ_c = \begin{pmatrix} σ_K^2I_{10} & 0 \\ 0 & I_{10} \end{pmatrix}. \] In the finite datasets, the number of examples assigned to each cluster is equal whenever the sample size is divisible by \(K\) and otherwise differs by at most one.

Mixed local prediction rules. Each cluster receives a nonlinear link function independently and uniformly, with replacement, from
\[ \mathcal{G} =\Bigg\{ t \mapsto \frac{t^2 − 1}{\sqrt{2}},\quad t\mapsto \sin(t),\quad t \mapsto \tanh(t)\Bigg\} \tag{5} \] Sampling with replacement means that two clusters may receive the same link. For a sample from cluster \(c\), the response is
\[ y = g_c(⟨x, v_c⟩) + \varepsilon,\quad \varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 0.02^2). \tag{6} \] Since \(v_c\) is supported only on the predictive coordinates,
\[ ⟨x, v_c⟩ = \tilde{v}_c^\top z_{pred}. \] Thus, both the relevant predictive direction vc and the nonlinear response function \(g_c\) may change across clusters.

Training and test sets. For each K and seed, we generate a training pool containing 200,000 examples. We use the nested training sizes
\[ n ∈ \{20,000, 50,000, 100,000, 200,000\}, \] so that the dataset at a smaller value of n is contained in every larger training set. Each run uses a separate test set of 4,096 examples. All methods evaluated for the same \(K, n\), and seed use exactly the same training and test examples.

We use five seeds indexed by \(r ∈ \{0,...,4\}\). For a fixed K, the cluster centers and predictive directions are shared across the five runs. The link assignments, training and test samples, label noise, and model initialization vary across runs. Specifically, the model and mixed-link seed is \(1000 + r\), the training-pool seed is \(12345 + r\), and the test-set seed is \(54321 + r\). We report the mean over these five runs, with shaded regions showing one empirical standard deviation.

MLP architectures and optimization. We evaluate one-hidden-layer ReLU, GELU, ReGLU, and SwiGLU networks, all with width 4,096. For ReLU and GELU, the hidden representation has the form
\[ h(x) = \phi(W x + b). \] For the gated architectures, it has the form
\[ h(x) = \phi(W_gx + b_g) \odot (W_vx + b_v), \] where \(\phi\) is ReLU for ReGLU and SiLU for SwiGLU. All hidden weights, readout weights, and biases are trainable.

We optimize the networks using Adam with batch size 4,096, cosine learningrate decay, and zero weight decay. The activation-specific schedules are
\[ \begin{array}{c | c} \text{Activation} & \text{Learning-rate schedule} \\ \hline \text{ReLU} & 10^{−2} → 10^{−4} \\ \text{GELU} & 3 × 10^{−2} → 3 × 10^{−4} \\ \text{ReGLU} & 3 × 10^{−3} → 3 × 10^{−5} \\ \text{SwiGLU} & 10^{−2} → 10^{−4} \end{array} \] Training continues for at least 20,000 updates and for at most 200,000 updates. After the minimum number of updates, optimization stops when either the training MSE reaches
\[ 4 × 10^{−4} \] , which equals the label-noise variance, or the training loss plateaus. The plateau rule stops after 60 consecutive evaluations, performed every 500 updates, without a relative training-MSE improvement of at least \(5 × 10^{−4}\) . We restore the checkpoint attaining the lowest training MSE. Stopping and checkpoint selection use training MSE only and never use test performance.

Global Laplace and RFM. RFM uses the metric-dependent Laplace kernel
\[ k_t(x, x^\prime) = \exp\Bigg(−\frac{||M_t^{1/2}(x − x^\prime)||_2}{h_t}\Bigg), \tag{7} \] where \(M_t\) is the metric at RFM iteration t and ht is the kernel bandwidth. The initial metric is
\[ M_0 = I_{20}, \] so iteration zero is the isotropic Laplace-kernel baseline.

For numerical stability across different values of \(K\), the bandwidth is computed from within-cluster distances in the transformed space. We estimate a median pairwise distance separately within each cluster and take the median of these cluster-level values. The bandwidth is recomputed after every metric update.

This bandwidth calibration uses the cluster identities, but only to select one scalar bandwidth shared by the entire global kernel. The global Laplace and RFM predictors are still fit jointly to all training examples, do not use the cluster identities in their regression objective, and do not use them when making test predictions. Thus, they do not fit separate cluster-specific predictors.

After each kernel fit, RFM estimates the average gradient outer product
\[ \hat{G}_t =\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^m∇\hat{f}_t(x_i)∇\hat{f}_t(x_i)^\top \] using at most \(m = 20,000\) training examples. A normalized version of \(\hat{G}_t\) becomes the metric for the next iteration. We perform three metric updates, producing iterations 0, 1, 2, 3.

Exact kernel solves are used for fewer than 11,000 training examples and include a ridge parameter of \(10^{−6}\) . Larger problems are solved using EigenPro [Ma et al., 2018, Ma and Belkin, 2019, Abedsoltan et al., 2023, 2025] without an explicit ridge penalty. EigenPro optimization stops upon reaching training MSE \(4×10^{−4}\) or its optimization limit. Its effective regularization therefore arises primarily from early stopping.

The displayed global RFM curve reports the smallest test MSE among iterations 0:3. It is therefore an optimistic diagnostic envelope rather than a deployable iteration-selection procedure.

Per-cluster oracle methods. The per-cluster Laplace and RFM oracle methods are given the true cluster identity during both training and testing. They partition the training data by cluster, fit one independent predictor within each partition, and evaluate each test example using the predictor associated with its true cluster. The reported MSE is pooled across all test examples.

A.3 Additional Details for Trained MLPs Encode Cluster Structures

This appendix describes how we extract cluster gates from a trained ReLU MLP and reuse them to construct independent local Laplace and RFM predictors. The experiment uses the same mixed-link clustered distribution as Section 3.2.

Mixed-link clustered data. We decompose the ambient space into ten clusterposition coordinates and ten predictive coordinates,
\[ \mathbb{R}^{20} = \mathcal{S}_{gate} \oplus \mathcal{S}_{pred}. \] We consider \(K ∈ \{2, 5, 10, 50\}\) equally represented clusters. Each cluster \(c\) has a unit cluster-position direction \(s_c ∈ \mathbb{S}^9\) and an independently generated unit predictive direction \(v_c ∈ \mathbb{S}^9\).

The directions sc are selected by farthest-point sampling from a large set of random unit vectors. Let
\[ ρ_K = \max\limits_{c\neq c^\prime}s_c^\top s_{c^\prime} \] be the largest pairwise inner product. We set the cluster-center radius to \(R = 1\) and choose the standard deviation in the cluster-position coordinates as
\[ σ_K =\frac{R(1 − ρ_K)}{2γ},\quad γ = 4. \] Thus, the noise level adapts to the closest pair of cluster centers, keeping the clusters well separated as \(K\) changes.

For an example from cluster \(c\), we sample
\[ z_{gate}, z_{pred} \overset{iid}{\sim}\mathcal{N}(0, I_{10}) \] and construct
\[ x = (Rs_c + σ_Kz_{gate}, z_{pred}). \tag{8} \] The first ten coordinates therefore identify the cluster, while the last ten coordinates contain the variables used for prediction.

Each cluster independently receives a nonlinear response function gc, drawn uniformly from
\[ \mathcal{G} =\Bigg\{t \mapsto \frac{t^2 − 1}{\sqrt{2}},\quad t \mapsto \sin(t),\quad t \mapsto \tanh(t)\Bigg\}. \tag{9} \] The response is
\[ y = g_c\big(v_c^\top z_{pred}\big)+\varepsilon,\quad \varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N} (0, 0.02^2). \tag{10} \] Consequently, both the predictive direction \(v_c\) and the nonlinear response function \(g_c\) may differ across clusters. The link functions are sampled independently, so a particular realization need not contain all three functions, especially when \(K\) is small.

Datasets and repetitions. For every \(K\), we generate a balanced training pool containing 200,000 examples and use nested prefixes of sizes
\[ n ∈ \{20,000, 50,000, 100,000, 200,000\}. \] Evaluation uses an independently generated test set containing 4,096 examples. Within each run, exactly the same serialized training and test tensors are used by the MLP, global kernel methods, oracle methods, and MLP-gated methods.

Results are averaged over five seeds. For a fixed \(K\), the cluster-position and predictive directions are held fixed, while each seed independently resamples the cluster-specific link assignments, training and test examples, label noise, and MLP initialization. No test labels are used to construct the gates.

Source ReLU MLP. For each K, sample size, and seed, we train a onehidden-layer ReLU network of width 4,096,
\[ \hat{f}(x) =\sum_{j=1}^{4096}a_j \text{ReLU}(w_j^\top x + b_j ) + b_{out}. \] All weights and biases are trainable. Optimization uses Adam with minibatches of size 4,096, zero weight decay, and cosine learning-rate decay from \(10^{−2}\) to \(10^{−4}\). Training runs for at least 20,000 steps and at most 200,000 steps. It terminates when the full training MSE reaches \(4 × 10^{−4}\), or when the training loss plateaus. We restore the checkpoint with the smallest full training MSE. Neither test MSE nor cluster identity is used for checkpoint selection.

Selecting active neurons. After training, we freeze the MLP. For neuron \(j\), define its average output-weighted activation on the training set as
\[ m_j =\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n|a_j| \text{ReLU}(w_j^\top x_i + b_j ). \tag{11} \] We apply two-means clustering to \(\{\log_{10} m_j\}_{j=1}^{4096}\) and retain the group with the larger average contribution. The resulting active-neuron set is denoted by \(\mathcal{J}\). If fewer than \(K\) neurons are retained, we instead keep the \(K\) neurons with the largest \(m_j\).

Contribution profiles and learned gates. For every training input \(x\), we compute its normalized contribution profile over the active neurons:
\[ p_j(x) =\frac{|a_j| \text{ReLU}(w_j^\top x + b_j)}{\sum_{ℓ∈\mathcal{J}} |a_ℓ| \text{ReLU}(w_ℓ^\top x + b_ℓ)},\quad j ∈ \mathcal{J}. \tag{12} \] Thus \(p(x)\) describes which active first-layer neurons contribute to the MLP prediction on input \(x\), independent of the overall magnitude of the prediction.

We apply \(K\)-means++ to the training contribution profiles and use \(M = K\) learned gates. Knowledge of the number of clusters is therefore provided, but the true cluster assignments are never observed. We use eight initializations and at most 30 Lloyd iterations, retaining the solution with the smallest training inertia. Every training example is assigned to exactly one gate through its nearest centroid.

Let \(q_c\) denote the centroid of learned gate \(c\), with coordinate \(q_{cj}\) corresponding to active neuron \(j\). Although examples receive hard gate assignments, the centroid coordinates provide a soft association between neurons and gates: the same neuron may contribute to several gates.

Constructing gate-specific features. For each learned gate c, we form the positive-semidefinite metric
\[ G_c =\frac{\sum_{j∈\mathcal{J}}q_{cj}w_jw_j^\top}{\sum_{j∈\mathcal{J}}q_{cj}. \tag{13} \] The corresponding gate-specific representation is
\[ z_c(x) = G_c^{1/2} x. \tag{14} \] The MLP biases affect the contribution profiles and hence the gate assignments, while \(G_c\) itself is constructed from the incoming first-layer weight directions.

For every learned gate, we project only the training examples assigned to that gate and fit an independent local Laplace or RFM predictor in the resulting representation.

Test-time prediction. For a new test input \(x\), we:

  • 1. pass \(x\) through the frozen MLP and compute its contribution profile \(p(x)\);

  • 2. assign \(x\) to the nearest fixed training centroid;

  • 3. transform it using the corresponding representation \(z_c(x) = G_c^{1/2} x\); and

  • 4. evaluate the local predictor fitted for that learned gate.

The test input is therefore assigned using only its MLP contribution profile. Neither its response nor its true cluster identity is used.

Local Laplace and RFM predictors. The local Laplace predictor for gate \(c\) uses
\[ k_c(x, x^\prime) = exp\Bigg(−\frac{||G_c^{1/2} (x − x^\prime)||_2}{h_c}\Bigg), \] where \(h_c\) is initialized using the median pairwise distance within that gate. We use ridge parameter \(10^{−6}\).

The local RFM begins from this Laplace kernel and performs three metric updates, producing iterations 0, 1, 2, 3, where iteration 0 is the local Laplace predictor. After each update, the bandwidth is recomputed using distances under the updated metric. Kernel systems with fewer than 11,000 local training examples are solved directly; larger systems use EigenPro. The same procedure is used for the global and oracle RFM baselines.

Compared methods. Figure 4 compares:

  • • one global RFM fitted to all training examples;

  • • MLP-gated local Laplace, using the gates and projections extracted from the ReLU MLP;

B.1 Proof of Theorem 1

We first prove Theorem 1 invoking Lemmas 3 to 9, whose statements and proofs are provided after the main proof. A roadmap of their dependencies is shown in fig. 7.

Throughout this subsection, the normalized means and predictive directions
\[ \frac{µ_1}{R},...,\frac{µ_K}{R}, v_1,..., v_K \] form an orthonormal basis of \(\mathbb{R}^d\), where \(d = 2K\). In the notation of Section 2, we may write
\[ c \sim \text{Unif}([K]),\quad x = µ_c + z,\quad z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_d), \] and \[ y = h_3\big(v_c^\top z\big),\quad h_3(t) = \frac{t^3 − 3t}{\sqrt{6}}. \] For \(ω ∈ \mathbb{S}^{d−1}\), define the weights’ correlations to the cluster means and predictive directions
\[ b_c(ω) = ⟨ω, µ_c⟩,\quad ρ_c(ω) = ⟨ω, v_c⟩. \] Write the ReLU activation function and the Gaussian p.d.f. as
\[ \phi(t) = t_+, \phi(t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2π}}e^{−t^2/2}, \] and define the unsigned and signed population-correlation objectives, respectively, by
\[ Φ(ω) = \mathbb{E}\big[y \phi(ω^\top x)\big],\quad Ψ_ζ(ω) = ζΦ(ω),\quad ζ ∈ \{±1\}. \] Proof of Theorem 1. For each initial direction \(ω_j^0\), let (\overline{u}_j , \overline{ω}_j )\) be the corresponding self-selected teacher-only trajectory from Lemma 7.

By Lemmas 7 and 5, almost surely there are a cluster label \(J_j ∈ [K]\) and an orientation \(τ_j ∈ \{±1\}\) such that
\[ \lim \sup\limits_{t→∞}||\overline{ω}_j(t) − τ_jv_{J_j}||_2 \leq \frac{C}{R} \] for a universal constant \(C\).

Since \(m \lt ∞\) and every teacher-only direction converges, for every \(δ \gt 0\) there is a finite time \(T_δ\) such that, simultaneously for all \(j ∈ [m]\,
\[ ||\overline{ω}_j(T_δ) − τ_jv_{J_j}||_2 \leq \frac{δ}{2}+\frac{C}{R}. \] Apply Lemma 9 on \([0, T_δ]\). For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\),

\[ \Bigg|\Bigg|\frac{w_j(T_δ)}{||w_j(T_δ)||_2}− τ_jv_{J_j}\Bigg|\Bigg|_2 \leq δ +\frac{C}{R}. \] The independence and uniformity of the labels \(J_1,...,J_m\), together with the cluster-coverage probability, follow from Lemma 8.

B.1.1 The Population-correlation Landscape

In this subsection, we study the critical points of the optimization problem
\max\limits_{ω∈\mathbb{S}^{d−1}}Φ_ζ(ω). \tag{15} \] The use of homogeneity to characterize parameter directions is part of a broader literature on the implicit bias of gradient methods, beginning with homogeneous linear predictors and extending to homogeneous neural networks [Soudry et al., 2018, Ji and Telgarsky, 2019, Lyu and Li, 2020, Ji and Telgarsky, 2020]. These results motivate the directional viewpoint used here, but do not directly yield the squared-loss, small-initialization approximation in our setting; we establish the required approximation directly in Lemma 9. (In the subsequent section of the appendix, we will show how the sign ζ is determined for each neuron at very early times of training.) We show that the critical points of equation 15 are specialized to clusters of the data distribution.

First, we provide an explicit formula for the signed population-correlation objective in terms of a weight’s correlations with the cluster means and predictive directions. This lemma is stated for weights \(ω\) on the unit sphere, but is later applied to small weights ω because of the homogeneity of ReLU networks.

Lemma 3 (Cubic correlation identity). For every \(ω ∈ \mathbb{S}^{d−1}\) and \(ζ ∈ \{±1\}\),
\[ Ψ_ζ(ω) = −\frac{ζ}{K\sqrt{6}}\sum_{c=1}^K b_c(ω)φ\big(b_c(ω)\big)ρ_c(ω)^3. \] Proof. Fix \(ω ∈ \mathbb{S}^{d−1}\). Conditional on \(c\),
\[ ω^\top x = b_c(ω) + ω^\top z,\quad y = h_3\big(v_c^\top z\big). \] Hence the contribution of cluster \(c\) to \(Φ(ω) = \mathbb{E}\big[ y\phi(ω^\top x)\big]\) is
\[ \mathbb{E}\Big[h_3\big(v_c^\top z\big)\big(b_c(ω) + ω^\top z\big)_+\Big] \] Set \[ U = v_c^\top z,\quad V = ω^\top z,\quad ρ = ρ_c(ω) = ⟨v_c, ω⟩. \] Since \(z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_d)\) and \(v_c, ω\) are unit vectors, \(U\) and \(V\) are standard Gaussian, and
\[ \mathbb{E}[UV] = v_c^\top \mathbb{E}[zz^\top]ω = ⟨v_c, ω⟩ = ρ. \] Thus \((U, V)\) is a jointly standard Gaussian pair with correlation \(ρ\).

For \(|ρ| \lt 1\), define
\[ Z :=\frac{U − ρV}{\sqrt{1 − ρ^2}} \] Because \((U, V)\) is jointly Gaussian, \((Z, V)\) is also jointly Gaussian. Moreover,
\[ \mathbb{E}[Z] = 0,\quad \mathbb{E}[Z^2] = 1, \] and \[ \mathbb{E}[ZV] = \frac{\mathbb{E}[UV] − ρ\mathbb{E}[V^2]}{\sqrt{1 − ρ^2}}= 0. \] Jointly Gaussian random variables with zero covariance are independent, so \(Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)\) is independent of \(V\). Therefore
\[ U = ρV +\sqrt{1 − ρ^2} Z. \] When \(|ρ| = 1\), the same representation holds with the second term equal to zero.

Conditioning on \(V\) and using the independence of \(Z\) gives
\[ \mathbb{E}[U|V] = ρV \] and \[ \begin{align} \mathbb{E}[U^3|V] &= E\Big[\big(ρV +\sqrt{1 − ρ^2} Z\big)^3\Big|V\Big]\\ \\ &= ρ^3V^3 + 3ρ(1 − ρ^2)V, \end{align} \] since \(\mathbb{E}[Z] = \mathbb{E}[Z^3] = 0\) and \(\mathbb{E}[Z^2] = 1\). Recalling that
\[ h_3(x) = \frac{x^3 − 3x}{\sqrt{6}}, \] we obtain
\[ \begin{align} \mathbb{E}[h_3(U)|V] &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\big(\mathbb{E}[U^3 |V] − 3\mathbb{E}[U|V]\big) \\ \\ &= ρ^3h_3(V). \end{align} \] Since \((b_c(ω) + V )_+\) depends only on \(V\), the tower property now gives
\[ \begin{align} \mathbb{E}\Big[h_3(U)\big(b_c(ω) + V\big)_+\Big] &= \mathbb{E} \Big[\big(b_c(ω) + V\big)_+\mathbb{E}[h_3(U)|V]\Big] \\ \\ &= ρ_c(ω)^3\mathbb{E}_{G\sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)}\Big[h_3(G)\big( b_c(ω) + G\big)_+\Big]. \end{align} \] It remains to compute the one-dimensional expectation. For any \(b ∈ \mathbb{R}\),
\[ \mathbb{E}[h_3(G)(b + G)_+] = \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\int_{-b}^∞(x^3 − 3x)(b + x)φ(x) dx. \] Using \[ \frac{d}{dx}\big[(x^2 − 1)φ(x)\big]= −(x^3 − 3x)φ(x), \] integration by parts yields
\[ \int_{-b}^∞ (x^3 − 3x)(b + x)φ(x) dx =\int_{-b}^∞(x^2 − 1)φ(x) dx, \] where the boundary term vanishes because \(b + x = 0\) at \(x = −b\) and \(φ(x) → 0\) as \(x → ∞\). Since
